NASA는 향후 아르테미스 계획의 후속 임무에서도 이 카메라를 계속 사용할 계획이며, 특히 예정된 유인 달 표면 재도착 임무에 활용될 예정입니다. 이는 인류가 50년 이상 만에 다시 달을 밟게 되는 역사적인 순간이 될 것입니다.

Special Space Cameras Contributing to the Artemis II Mission

According to IT House, the German Helmholtz Institute (GSI) and the Facility for Advanced Ion Research (FAIR) have made significant contributions to the success of NASA's Artemis II lunar exploration mission. Based on Nikon Z9, Verified Radiation Resistance in Extreme Environments

The camera is based on a modified Nikon Z9 model. In March 2025, NASA conducted comprehensive radiation resistance tests on this camera at the GSI and FAIR particle accelerator facilities. During the testing process, the camera was exposed to high-energy heavy ion irradiation designed to simulate space environments.

The objective of these tests was to verify the operational performance and reliability of equipment in extreme conditions. The results confirmed that the camera remained stable and reliable even in intense radiation environments, successfully meeting all requirements necessary for lunar exploration missions.

Proven Performance in Actual Space Environments

After passing rigorous testing, this camera was deployed in NASA's recently completed Artemis II mission, where it was used to capture numerous stunning images. These include photos of solar eclipses captured from space, providing clear evidence that the equipment performed exceptionally well in actual space operation environments.

NASA plans to continue using this camera in subsequent missions of the Artemis program, particularly for upcoming manned lunar surface return missions. This will mark a historic moment as humanity sets foot on the moon again for the first time in over 50 years.

Expert Comments: The Roles of GSI and FAIR

Professor Thomas Nelson, Scientific Director of GSI and FAIR, stated, "Our accelerator facilities can accurately simulate space radiation environments on Earth. This allows us to play a crucial role in ensuring the safety and performance of equipment for future space missions."

Professor Marco Durant, Head of Biophysics at GSI and FAIR, added, "Based on our expertise in radiation physics, we are collaborating with NASA and the European Space Agency (ESA) to contribute to the advancement of international space science research. Our research not only supports the development of high-reliability aerospace technology but also helps humanity better understand the effects of space radiation on the human body."

Tim Wagner, a physicist at GSI and FAIR and the head of microelectronics radiation resistance testing at the institute, emphasized, "GSI and FAIR are unique test facilities in Europe capable of performing irradiation tests on electronic components, especially commercial off-the-shelf parts, using high-energy heavy ions. This is a core technology assurance factor essential for all future space missions."

The specialized camera, based on the Nikon Z9, proved its high reliability in extreme space environments, establishing a vital technical foundation for humanity's future deep-space activities, including manned lunar exploration.

According to IT Home, Germany's Helmholtz Center for Heavy Ion Research (GSI) and the Facility for Advanced Ion Research (FAIR) provided critical contributions to NASA's Artemis II lunar exploration mission. These institutions conducted a series of tests on specialized cameras developed to withstand actual space environments using their particle accelerator facilities.

Nikon Z9-Based Radiation Resistance Verification

The camera is based on the Nikon Z9 model and underwent comprehensive radiation resistance testing at GSI and FAIR's particle accelerator facilities in March 2025. During the testing process, the camera was exposed to high-energy heavy ion irradiation designed to simulate the harsh conditions of outer space.

The primary objective of this test was to verify the operational performance and reliability of equipment in extreme environments. The results confirmed that the camera remained stable and reliable even under intense radiation, successfully meeting all requirements necessary for lunar exploration missions.

Proven Performance in Actual Space Environments

After passing rigorous testing, these cameras were deployed in NASA's recently completed Artemis II mission, where they captured numerous high-quality images. These include photos of a solar eclipse taken from space, providing clear evidence that the equipment performed exceptionally well in actual space operations.

NASA plans to continue using these cameras for future missions under the Artemis program, specifically for upcoming manned lunar surface return missions. This will mark a historic moment as humanity prepares to walk on the moon again for the first time in over 50 years.

Expert Comments: The Role of GSI and FAIR

Professor Thomas Nelson, Scientific Director at GSI and FAIR, stated, "Our accelerator facilities can accurately simulate space radiation environments on Earth, playing a vital role in ensuring the safety and performance of equipment for future space missions."

Professor Marco Durant, Head of Biophysics at GSI and FAIR, added, "Based on our expertise in radiation physics, we collaborate with NASA and the European Space Agency (ESA) to contribute to international space science research. Our work not only supports the development of high-reliability aerospace technology but also helps us better understand the effects of space radiation on the human body."

Tim Wagner, a physicist at GSI and FAIR and head of microelectronics radiation resistance testing, emphasized, "GSI and FAIR possess unique testing facilities in Europe capable of performing irradiation tests on electronic components—particularly commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) parts—using high-energy heavy ions. This is a critical factor for ensuring the reliability of all future space missions."
